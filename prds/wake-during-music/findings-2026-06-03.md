## Fix attempts and what they revealed

### A. Enable Speex AEC (`aec_enabled=true`)

- Added to dev `config.json`; service restart.
- Startup log:
  ```
  AEC reference reader started | monitor_source=alsa_output.platform-soc_sound.stereo-fallback.monitor
  AEC pipeline started | rate=16000 frame_size=160 filter_length=1600 reference_delay_samples=1280
  AEC calibration: too few mic samples collected | collected_bytes=7680 target_bytes=105600
  ```
- Calibration starvation: targeted 1.1 s of mic capture (105 600 bytes
  at 48 kHz / s16le / 2-byte frames), got exactly **one** 80 ms
  AudioBus chunk (7680 bytes) before the `queue.get` 200 ms empty
  timeout fired. Default 80 ms delay retained.
- During Spotify playback with the 80 ms delay (real Pi acoustic delay
  is in the 100-240 ms range per the original AEC commit):
  | t         | mic_rms | ref_rms | cleaned_rms | suppression_dB |
  |-----------|---------|---------|-------------|----------------|
  | 15:41:05  | 540     | 710     | 568         | -0.4           |
  | 15:41:15  | 1081    | 1549    | 1127        | -0.4           |
  | 15:41:20  | 3354    | 3513    | 3796        | -1.1           |
  | 15:41:25  | 4471    | 3892    | 1819        | **+7.8**       |
  | 15:41:30  | 2021    | 2562    | 2044        | -0.1           |
  | 15:41:35  | 1641    | 3601    | 1612        | +0.2           |
  Filter never converges; negative suppression means Speex is adding
  energy. Wake attempts during this period scored 0.34-0.45 (model
  couldn't see "Hey Jarvis" through the bleed even with AEC running).

### B. Manual `aec_reference_delay_ms = 150`, calibration off

- Set both keys in `config.json`; restart. New
  `reference_delay_samples = 2400`.
- Outcome: same swing between -2 and +4 dB suppression. Pinning the
  delay near the middle of the Pi range did not rescue Speex. Confirms
  the calibration starvation isn't the only issue with this algorithm.

### C. Lower `wake_music_trust_score` 0.95 → 0.75

- Reverted (A) and (B); added the override; restart.
- During loud Spotify (baseline_rms 11k-13k):
  ```
  wake-suppressed-music-bleed | score=0.142  rms=13464  baseline=13464  energy_floor=20196
  wake-suppressed-music-bleed | score=0.156  rms=15186  baseline=13685  energy_floor=20528
  wake-suppressed-music-bleed | score=0.252  rms=8892   baseline=11736  energy_floor=17603
  wake-suppressed-music-bleed | score=0.429  rms=8302   baseline=11074  energy_floor=16611
  wake-suppressed-music-bleed | score=0.39   rms=8836   baseline=11026  energy_floor=16540
  ```
  OWW scores capped at 0.14-0.43 because the model can barely see
  "Hey Jarvis" through the bleed. Trust score moved from 0.95 to 0.75
  rescues nothing in this regime — the score never crosses 0.5, let
  alone 0.75 or 0.95.
- Single wake-music-trust-score-bypass observed at `score=0.958,
  trust_score=0.75` — but 0.958 ≥ 0.95 means the old default would
  have fired it too. No evidence the 0.75 unlocked anything new.

### D. Drop mic PGA 60 % → 49 % (level 71 → 58, +35.5 dB → +29 dB)

- The breakthrough finding of the session.
- `parec` capture of music-only at PGA 60 % showed digital clipping:
  ```
  === MIC (PGA 60 %, music only, 15 s) ===
  Pk lev dB  0.00      Min -1.000000   Max  0.999969
  Bit-depth  16/16     Pk count  328 (L 22, R 634)
  Flat factor 13.90 (severe flat-topped clipping)
  ```
  At install-default gain the ADC saturates on music alone. Voice
  arriving on top of music has no headroom — peaks are flat-topped
  digital ones and zeros, irreversibly lost.
- Same recording at PGA 49 %:
  ```
  === MIC (PGA 49 %, music only, 15 s) ===
  Pk lev dB  -8.49     Bit-depth  15/16
  Pk count   2         Flat factor  0.00
  ```
  Clean signal. -6.5 dB analog gain reduction = 8.5 dB of digital
  headroom recovered.
- Live wake during music after gain drop fired several times in the
  16:35 minute (scores 0.338-0.792 with music_mode true, RMS profiles
  showing voice clearly above music baseline).

### E. Drop further to PGA 42 % (level 50, +25 dB) + `wake_word_music_energy_multiplier 1.5 → 1.2`

- Multiple `Wake fired` events at score 0.94-0.99 **with
  `pre_wake_speech_seconds = 0.0`** — no voice activity in the
  5-second pre-wake window. These are **music-only false-wakes**.
  The model is pattern-matching the wake phrase against music alone
  once the mic signal has enough headroom for music transients to
  look like phonemes.
- Each music false-wake triggered the CC roundtrip → `not_for_me`
  (no real speech to transcribe) → `record_not_for_me_event` →
  20 s cooldown → second false-wake within 30 s escalated to 60 s.
  Real user "Hey Jarvis" attempts during the cooldown logged as
  `wake-suppressed-gate | cooldown_remaining_sec=30.3 → 10.8 → 2.5`
  with OWW scores **0.95-0.99** (model was hearing the user
  perfectly; the gate was blocking).
- User report: "I'm trying but it's not waking" — exactly the
  user-visible failure mode the cooldown produces.
- Conclusion: PGA is a tradeoff dial. Too high clips real voice;
  too low lets music spoof the wake phrase. There is no PGA value
  that makes a single-mic OWW front-end robust to both.

Voice+music clipping at PGA 49 % tells us the dynamic range of
real-world voice + music exceeds the ADC range even at this reduced
gain. For loud-music + close-distance voice we may need PGA below
49 %, or the algorithm has to tolerate occasional clipped frames.

## Useful diagnostic: `pre_wake_speech_seconds`

The `Wake fired` log includes `pre_wake_speech_seconds` — the count of
frames in the 5 s pre-wake window where VAD detected speech-energy.
Real user wakes today had values 0.24 – 2.08. Every music false-wake
had value 0.0 or 0.08. This is the cleanest in-band signal we have
for distinguishing real vs spoofed wakes from journal data alone.

Not a fix on its own (user explicitly rejected the "gate on
pre_wake_speech" patch), but it's the right diagnostic to instrument
Phase 5 validation around.

## What this implies for the algorithm

1. The room transfer function from `music_only_mic.wav` vs
   `music_only_ref.wav` is the cleanest single dataset for modelling
   the speaker → mic acoustic path. If our algorithm can produce a
   filter that reconstructs the mic from the reference, we know AEC
   has the bones to work — independent of the broken Speex
   calibration path.
2. Recording 3's clipping is a data point: at any PGA where music
   alone is clean, voice + music can still saturate. Either we accept
   that and design the algorithm around clipped frames, or PGA goes
   lower and we double down on robust voice-energy detection so the
   model isn't tricked by background noise.
3. The 30 mm mic baseline is small but real — mic-L vs mic-R crest
   factors differed slightly (8.50 vs 7.36 in the voice-only take).
   Phase 2 has to quantify whether stereo input is meaningfully
   useful, or whether AEC3-with-mono-mic-plus-reference is just as
   good for less complexity.

## Open questions for Phase 2

- Cross-correlation lag between `music_only_mic.wav` and
  `music_only_ref.wav` — what's the *actual* per-Pi acoustic delay,
  to within ±1 ms?
- Coherence(L-mic, R-mic) — how independent are the two channels
  really? If γ² ≥ 0.95 below 4 kHz, stereo gives us nothing new.
- PSD overlap of voice vs music in this kitchen — does spectral
  subtraction have any room to work, or is the overlap too complete?
- Reverb tail length (RT60 estimate from the room response): tells
  us the minimum AEC filter length we need.
